and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
A page with the directness of a street poster: flat saturated colour, the heaviest type you can find, short blunt statements, and photographs cut out and stuck on. It is for subjects with a voice. Write the page as things a person would actually say out loud, and let the design say them at full volume. The craft is in the control beneath the noise: a strict grid, two or three colours, and exact alignment keep it from becoming a mess.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
Hero: a statement of three to six words in ultra-heavy type at 11–15vw, filling the width, lines stacked tight (leading 0.85), flush left with 20–28px margins, on a flat saturated ground. One cut-out or hard-cropped photograph overlaps the type from the right or bottom, tilted 3–6 degrees. A single chunky button in the contrasting flat colour. Sections are solid panels in alternating palette colours: one is a row of short statements each on its own panel of unequal width; one is a single photograph bleeding off two edges with a caption block set over a solid colour bar; one is a large plain list. Panels meet edge to edge. Corners are square or use one large radius (28–40px) consistently.

## 3. Type — Chillax
A soft geometric sans with wide round letters and relaxed joins, warm at semibold and easy at text size. Suits consumer apps, community products, cafes and small friendly brands.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://api.fontshare.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://cdn.fontshare.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=chillax@400,500,600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Chillax** — weight 600 · tracking -0.03em · leading 1.0 · sentence case
- Text: **Chillax** — 400 for body, 500 for emphasis · leading 1.5 · tracking 0
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
The heaviest weight available, set tight, mostly lowercase or mostly uppercase (choose one). Body text is a plain grotesk at 18–20px.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 5. Imagery
**This subject is software and there are no screenshots, so its pictures are its own interface.** Wherever this sheet says photograph, image or object, build a believable fragment of the product in HTML at real size (real labels, numbers that add up, one item hovered or selected) by the app-screen rules (references/app-screens.md: deal its style once with `node <skill>/scripts/draw.mjs --app --kind <kind> --keep palette,type --out .taste-skill/app`, and build it with assets/app.css and assets/charts.js), put the attribute `data-ui` on its root, and crop or scale it the way the blueprint treats a photograph: a full-bleed close crop, a tile, the fixed half of a split. Where a fragment makes no sense, use a flat colour field with type on it. Never a fake dashboard of grey bars, never a device frame. If the product works on something physical (ships, plants, buildings, a city), one real picture of that world can carry a section: find it with `node C:/Users/PERSONAL/.claude/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "<that world>"`, download it with `--get`, credit it on the page, and never let it pass for the product or its customers.

Photographs with attitude, cropped hard or cut to a simple shape with `clip-path` (circle, arch, blunt polygon), slightly rotated, overlapping the type. Two or three on the page.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

## 6. Hero — Two-line statement, full width
Paper and ink only; no image. The wordmark at 15px sits top-left and two or three text links at the same size top-right, both on 24px margins; there is no bar. The statement fills the upper two thirds: two lines at clamp(96px, 12.5vw, 200px), medium to bold weight, tracking -0.05em, leading 0.86, starting 15% below the top edge. The first line is flush to the left margin and the second flush to the right margin, so the pair spans the full width and steps across the screen. Size the type so the longer line runs margin to margin: tune the vw value to the character count and test it at 1440 and 390. Between the statement and the bottom row about 18% of the height is left empty. The bottom row sits on one baseline 28px above the bottom edge: one supporting sentence at 22px on the left, max 44ch on two lines, and the single action bottom-right as a solid ink rectangle with square corners. There is no colour beyond ground and ink.

On a phone: Both lines turn flush left on 16px margins at 17vw, leading 0.9, starting at 14% of the height and filling the width. A gap of about 20% of the screen follows. The sentence sits at 17px above a full-width action, both anchored to the bottom with 20px to spare. Navigation reduces to the wordmark and one menu word.
Headline: A 3-5 word statement on two lines, at most 11 characters per line so 17vw type fits a phone.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. how-it-works — One card, four fields (strip)
Four flat colour fields stand side by side across the full viewport, each 25vw wide and 84svh tall, butted together with no gaps; neighbours differ clearly in lightness. The same card from the product (a ticket, an order, a request) appears once in each field at the same height, 38% down, centred, 260px wide at true size, each copy marked data-ui. It is further along every time: bare as it arrives, then with an owner and a due date, then with its checklist half ticked, then closed with its final figure. Along the bottom of each field, 28px in, one clause at clamp(22px, 2vw, 32px) on two lines begins with its order word: 'First it lands here.' 'Then someone takes it.' The heading, two to four words at the same size in the heaviest weight, sits top-left in the first field only. On hover a field's card rises 6px over 150ms.
On a phone: The fields stack, each full width and 64svh tall, colours meeting edge to edge. In each: the clause at 24px top-left with 20px margins, then the card at true size, left-aligned, 32px below. The heading sits in the first field above its clause, at 24px in the heaviest weight.

### 2. showcase — Headline you can type (statement)
The section's headline is an input. One line at clamp(48px, 8vw, 132px), tight tracking, flush left across columns 1–12, is a real `<input>` (root marked data-ui) with no border and no fill, prefilled with a believable two-to-five-word example of what a user gives the product: a search, a question, a date in words, a colour name. A solid caret block 0.08em wide blinks after the last character. 48px beneath, on the same left edge, the product's answer appears at true interface size, at most 560px wide: three result rows, one parsed value, or a swatch, updating as the visitor types. Bottom-left, three more examples at 17px act as plain text buttons that refill the line. The rest of the 88svh field is empty. If the text outgrows the line it scrolls inside the input; it never wraps.
On a phone: The input is set at 11vw on one line and scrolls sideways inside itself when the text runs long. The answer follows 28px below at full width and true size. The three examples become a wrapping row of filled blocks 44px tall beneath the answer; tapping one refills the line.

### 3. stats — Stepped figure tiles (grid)
Three figures on three tiles that refuse to line up. A tall tile in the accent colour fills columns 1–6 and is 70vh high; it holds one figure at clamp(56px, 5.4vw, 92px) in its top-left corner, 28px in, and its caption at 17px in its bottom-left corner, with empty colour between. On the right, an ink-coloured tile in columns 7–9 starts 14vh lower and is 44vh high: a paper-coloured figure top-left, a caption along its bottom edge. A third tile in a mid tone takes columns 10–12, starts 26vh down and is 34vh high, with the same internal layout. Gaps are 8px. The unequal heights leave notches of bare ground above and below the right-hand tiles; the heading, two to four words at 28–36px, sits in the upper notch on column 7. If the page has a motif, cut one corner of each tile to its geometry with clip-path.
On a phone: The tall tile comes first, at full width and 56vw high, with its figure at 15vw. The two smaller tiles follow side by side with a 6px gap, the right one starting 32px lower than the left, each with its figure at 11vw and its caption at the bottom. The heading sits above at 26px.

### 4. cta — The idea again, inverted (statement)
The close flips the page's colours: ink becomes the ground and the ground becomes the ink, across a full-bleed field at least 90svh tall. The page's opening idea returns as a headline of three to five words at 9–11vw on two lines, leading 0.9, flush left, positioned in the upper two-thirds; it should echo the hero's wording, not repeat it. Along the bottom, on one shared baseline 40px above the edge: one supporting sentence at 20px in columns 1–5 and one rectangular button in the accent colour, right-aligned in columns 10–12, about 56px tall. Nothing else: no image, no second button, no logo. The field's top edge is a hard horizontal line where it meets the section above. If the footer follows on the same inverted field, leave 160px before it so the close still reads as its own screen.
On a phone: The same inverted field at 80svh. Headline at 15vw on two lines at the top. The sentence sits lower at 17px, and the button spans the full width at the bottom, 56px tall, with 20px margins. Nothing else is added.

### 5. footer — The mark, built large (band)
On a flat colour field, the brand closes the page as a construction rather than as set type. Across the full content width and about 34svh tall, the wordmark is built from blocks: each letter is knocked out of its own solid rectangle in a palette colour, the rectangles differ in height (60–100% of the band) and butt together with 4px gaps, bottoms on one line. Where the project has a simple geometric mark or motif, enlarge that instead, drawn as inline SVG from flat rectangles, circles and quarter-arcs with the same 4px gaps. Below, 56px lower: four columns of plain links at 15px across columns 1–8, and two addresses or contact lines in columns 9–12. A last row carries the copyright at the left and one small filled button at the right. Space and the block construction do all the separating.
On a phone: The block wordmark spans the full width at about 22svh tall; a long name breaks into two rows of blocks. Link columns go two across at 15px, contact lines beneath at full width, then copyright and the small button on one row.

## 8. Motion — Masked headline
On load, each line of the headline rises from behind its own baseline, about 900ms with a strong ease-out, the second line 110ms after the first; the supporting sentence and the action then fade up together. Nothing else on the page performs. Because headlines are two lines at most, you author the line breaks and wrap each line yourself. Recipe: 'Masked line reveal' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Colour mass, type mass, and overlap.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Headlines still obey the two-line rule: at 13vw that means very few words, which is correct for this voice. No outlines, no offset shadows, no sticker confetti. Rotation is for two or three images only, never for text blocks.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
